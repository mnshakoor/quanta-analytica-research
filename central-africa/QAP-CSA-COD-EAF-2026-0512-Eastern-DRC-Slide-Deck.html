05 // EVIDENCE
Evidence Summary
What We Know
  • ADF attacks have produced civilian harm in North Kivu and Ituri and include patterns of looting, burning, abduction, and medical-site threat reporting. [S2][S4]
  • CODECO and CRP-linked violence in Ituri has escalated around villages, FARDC positions, and MONUSCO civilian protection interventions. [S3][S15]
  • Food insecurity and displacement pressures are severe in conflict-affected eastern DRC. [S7][S8][S9]
What We Assess
  • ADF is not merely a background actor. It is an acute corridor and site-risk driver in Beni, Ituri, and northward movement zones.
  • Ituri is the most immediate localized-escalation hotspot for Bunia, Djugu, Irumu, and adjacent humanitarian corridors.
  • Fragmented violence can generate strategic humanitarian effects by blocking routes and overwhelming field presence.
What We Do Not Know
  • Whether attacks near humanitarian corridors are deliberate targeting or operational proximity.
  • The degree of centralized CRP command and Thomas Lubanga's operational relevance.
  • Whether mediators can enforce humanitarian access commitments across M23-controlled and contested areas.
Key Drivers and Binding Constraints
  • FACT: Severe food insecurity and displacement are magnifying consequences of local violence. [S7][S8]
  • ASSESS: Communal protection, resource control, retaliation, and bargaining leverage are the strongest near-term remobilization drivers.
  • Structural constraints: Weak state reach, road dependency, terrain, and fragmented command environments.
  • Operational constraints: Limited route verification, medical referral fragility, staff exposure, and inconsistent security backstops.
WSI SOURCE INDEPENDENCE NOTEThe five Key Judgments are supported by independent streams across wire reporting, UN/mission reporting, humanitarian coordination, rights investigation, conflict-event analysis, and technical food-security sources. Single-source claims are explicitly caveated and do not carry Key Judgments alone.
06 // ANALYSIS
Five-Layer Strategic Cognition Map
LAYERASSESSMENT
L1 FOUNDATIONSEastern DRC contains overlapping conflict geographies: Ituri communal violence, ADF areas in North Kivu and Ituri, M23 / AFC pressure in the Kivus, mineral corridors, and humanitarian hubs dependent on road, airport, and border access.
L2 MECHANISMSCore mechanisms are localized protection and retaliation cycles, opportunistic armed-group expansion, resource taxation, FARDC / MONUSCO coverage gaps, and M23 negotiation incentives.
L3 DYNAMICSViolence-displacement-access feedback is the key system: attacks displace civilians, displacement stresses aid nodes, armed actors tax or disrupt access, and security actors redeploy, creating new vacuums.
L4 LEVERAGELeverage points include local access negotiation, route segmentation, medical-site protection, checkpoint mapping, MONUSCO / FARDC posture tracking, and diplomatic pressure around access commitments.
L5 PARADIGMSBest-fit paradigm: fragmented conflict economy with convergent humanitarian effects. The system can behave as if coordinated even when actor command structures remain fragmented.
06 // ANALYSIS
Analysis of Competing Hypotheses
HYPOTHESISEVIDENCE FOREVIDENCE AGAINSTDIAG.VERDICT
H1: Localized competition over land, protection, and resource control. Ituri violence patterns and communal retaliation dynamics. [S3][S15] Does not fully explain M23 regional and mineral dynamics. 4 Supported in Ituri and local militia theaters.
H2: Opportunistic escalation triggered by negotiations, redeployments, and state weakness. ADF movement under pressure, M23 negotiation fragility, and FARDC / MONUSCO gaps. [S1][S4][S5][S13] Does not alone prove deliberate humanitarian targeting. 5 Strong mechanism.
H3: Broader coordination among armed actors to weaken peace negotiations. Temporal overlap across theaters could create apparent convergence. No verified shared command channels or synchronized multi-province humanitarian targeting. 4 Least supported. Retain as watch hypothesis.
H4: External patronage and mineral networks are primary drivers. Strong for M23 / Rwanda and Rubaya-related mineral rents. [S10][S11][S12] Weaker evidence for CODECO, CRP-linked actors, and ADF as centrally driven by the same mechanism. 5 Strong for M23, limited for non-M23 groups.
H5: Fragmented violence produces strategic effects equivalent to coordination. Multiple actors simultaneously degrade access, displacement, and political bandwidth. [S2][S4][S7][S8][S14] Requires careful distinction between systemic convergence and command coordination. 5 Most supported overall.

09 // CONFIDENCE
Confidence and Uncertainties
OVERALL CONFIDENCE ASSESSMENT
MODERATE
  • Higher confidence for humanitarian severity, displacement scale, and broad conflict pattern assessments.
  • Higher confidence for M23 external-support findings corroborated across multiple independent streams.
  • Lower confidence for actor intent, internal command relationships, and exact casualty totals.
  • Lower confidence for whether corridor proximity constitutes deliberate humanitarian targeting.
Three Flip-Risk Uncertainties
FLIP-RISK 1Verified shared command channels emerge linking CODECO, CRP, ADF, and M23 in a coordinated targeting strategy against humanitarian corridors.
FLIP-RISK 2Synchronized attacks on aid corridors across provinces confirm deliberate humanitarian targeting rather than operational proximity.
FLIP-RISK 3Independently corroborated external support to multiple non-M23 militias emerges, fundamentally reshaping the conflict-economy assessment.
ANALYTIC CAVEATDirect deliberate targeting of humanitarian corridors by all militias remains insufficiently established. This product therefore assesses corridor pressure and access degradation rather than a confirmed coordinated anti-humanitarian campaign.

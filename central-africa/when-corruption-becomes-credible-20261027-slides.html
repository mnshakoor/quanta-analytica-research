When does government distrust make Ebola misinformation credible, and when does fear become a threat to health workers?

Full-scope Quanta Analytica Process™ strategic intelligence assessment, 27 October 2026.

SLIDE 02 / 12

Institutional legitimacy is a response capability.

Mistrust, perceived unfairness and medical fear can reinforce misinformation acceptance. However, the Bafwasende attack cannot be causally attributed to corruption or a centrally directed disinformation campaign.

SLIDE 03 / 12

Evidence must be time- and place-bounded.

WHO reported 9,704 cases and 4,687 deaths as of 20 October. Later national surveillance reported 10,060 cases and 4,887 deaths. These are sequential data points, not independent surveillance systems.

SLIDE 04 / 12

A fatal incident establishes a protection requirement.

Reuters and AP corroborate a nurse's killing and another worker's injury. Their accounts differ on whether the attack occurred October 23 or 24. Motive remains unresolved.

SLIDE 05 / 12

Perceived corruption is not proven misconduct.

The DRC scored 19/100 on the 2025 CPI. Historical Ebola studies describe alleged profiteering and response inequity. Neither observation proves diversion of 2026 Ebola response funds.

SLIDE 06 / 12

Credibility, governance and protection are linked.

KJ-1: Trust shapes information vulnerability. KJ-2: Perceived inequity can legitimate false claims. KJ-3: Violence requires a distinct explanation. KJ-4: Coordinated malicious influence is unproven. KJ-5: Joint monitoring and local co-governance merit testing.

SLIDE 07 / 12

Do not confuse correlation with causation.

H1 institutional mistrust; H2 service failures/inequities; H3 politicization and exclusion; H4 epidemic fear; H5 organized influence actor. Historical evidence challenges distrust-only explanations of mass violence; current Bafwasende motives remain unresolved.

SLIDE 08 / 12

A plausible reinforcing loop, not an estimated causal effect.

Perceived unfairness → institutional mistrust → acceptance of false medical claims → delayed care → response disruption → heightened grievance. A balancing pathway involves transparent procedures, community decision rights and reliable care.

SLIDE 09 / 12

Evidence quality is assessed at source and claim level.

14 source cards; seven RAPITIS dimensions; 16 atomic claim audits with corroboration, diagnosticity and falsifiability. Conflicting incident dates, denominator errors and unsupported actor attributions are explicitly quarantined.

SLIDE 10 / 12

Protect, verify and engage.

0–72 hours: investigate incident; protect staff; log suspended services. 3–30 days: audit compensation and procurement, measure trust, give local representatives meaningful decision roles. 30–180 days: evaluate interventions and institutionalize grievance protection.

SLIDE 11 / 12

Monitor thresholds, not just headlines.

Credible threats, service suspensions >24 hours, persistent below-target contact follow-up, recurrent false claims across independent channels and grievance-linked mobilization warrant operational review.

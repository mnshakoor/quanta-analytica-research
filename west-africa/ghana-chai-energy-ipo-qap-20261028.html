01 // BLUF

Bottom Line Up Front

BLUF-01 | [ASSESS] Ownership transfer is more strongly supported than new power-generation investment. Chai Energy publicly identifies two operating power projects as intended equity acquisitions. If the reported IPO purchases existing owners' shares, that consideration goes to selling shareholders, not into new generation capacity. The percentage of proceeds flowing to sellers versus new capital remains unknown. Confidence: Moderate on the mechanism; Low on the actual allocation. [1][2][18]

BLUF-02 | [FACT + ASSESS] One sponsor connects both targets, creating a consequential concentration question. Endeavor Energy is named among Bridge Power's sponsors and reported a 51.02% controlling interest in Twin City Energy in March 2026. This common ownership exposure is confirmed; whether Endeavor, Denham, Andaris, or a different investor is selling to Chai is not confirmed. The nexus warrants transaction-level related-party and valuation scrutiny, not an allegation of wrongdoing. Confidence: High on the disclosed nexus; Low on proposed seller identities. [3][4]

BLUF-03 | [FACT] Ghana's electricity-payment environment improved in 2025 but remains dependent on state support. ECG's audited payables to Early Power declined from GH¢1.261 billion to GH¢498.205 million and to Amandi Energy from GH¢2.937 billion to GH¢342.167 million between year-ends 2024 and 2025, a combined reduction of 79.98%. Those are balance-sheet stocks, not proof that the full decline represents cash payment; currency remeasurement, negotiated settlements, and accounting adjustments must also be tested. The IMF estimated a US$1.1 billion energy-sector financing gap for 2026. Confidence: High in reported figures; Moderate in the payment-risk inference. [7][8][9]

BLUF-04 | [ASSESS] The critical economic risk is a three-stage cash-conversion problem. The two plants must receive ECG/PPA settlements; project companies must clear operating, debt, reserve, and distribution restrictions; and Chai must fund the proposed preference-share obligations. A US-dollar-linked PPA at Twin City does not prove that US-dollar-indexed cash reaches Chai preference shareholders. Confidence: Moderate on mechanisms; Low on actual coverage. [1][4][5][7][11]

BLUF-05 | [FACT + ASSESS] The deal is not currently priced or underwritten from public evidence. No public SEC-approved Chai prospectus, final GSE admission document, authoritative pre-IPO capitalization table, executed acquisition agreements, current target-company cash-flow statements, or preference dividend/redemption covenants were located in the supplied research. The reported US$300 million is therefore an offer aspiration, not a confirmed fundraising result or equity valuation. Confidence: High that the research contains these gaps; this does not prove that no private documents exist. [1][18]

05 // EVIDENCE

Evidence Summary

5.1 What we know [FACT]

  1. B&FT reported Chai's ~US$300m proposal on 28 October 2026 on the basis of a transaction document it said it reviewed and a source close to the deal. The report is an original journalistic stream, not a regulator's approval. bne IntelliNews repeats it and does not constitute another independent confirmation of the headline amount. [1]
  2. Chai's own website names Bridge Power 202 MW and Twin City Energy 200 MW as acquisition targets, but a company intention is not a completed share transfer. [2]
  3. GE Vernova identifies Early Power Limited with the Bridge project, says sponsors include Endeavor Energy and Andaris Energy, and describes a 25-year PPA with ECG plus a government-backed Put/Call Option Agreement (PCOA) addressing specified termination-related obligations, not a blanket unconditional cash payment guarantee. [3][14]
  4. Endeavor's March 2026 release identifies Amandi Energy Limited trading as Twin City Energy, with Endeavor Energy Holdings LLC owning 51.02% and the plant operating under a USD-linked PPA with ECG. It flags steam-cycle recommissioning as a management priority. [4]
  5. AIIM historically disclosed an AIIF3 13.6% acquisition in Amandi and lists other historic sponsors. This is historical, not a verified current cap table. [5][6][16]
  6. ECG's 2025 audited accounts record year-end power-purchase payables of GH¢498.205m Early Power and GH¢342.167m Amandi; total ECG power-purchase payables were GH¢29.786bn, annual revenue GH¢22.109bn, net customer receivables ~GH¢12.236bn, operating cash outflow GH¢12.541bn, and purchased-versus-sold electricity system losses 26.88%. [7]
  7. The IMF reports a projected US$1.1bn shortfall in 2026, with continued dependence on payments, tariff updates and public-sector support. Its 2026 report explicitly notes that CWM disbursements did not yet cover all current bills. [8][9]
  8. SEC regulation of public issues and GSE admission require formal issuer, security, ownership and financial disclosures. The SEC source is identified on its cover as SEC/GUI/002/02/2023, correcting the 2022 date in the supplied PDF's reference [3]. The GSE rules are a February 2026 publication. [11][12][18]

5.2 What we assess [INFERENCE]

  • The proposed instrument adds holding-company distribution and legal-seniority risk above existing project and sovereign/off-taker exposure. [1][2][4][7]
  • The common Endeavor sponsorship raises the importance of testing seller concentration, common financing terms, valuation independence and relationships between issuer-side advisers and asset owners. It does not establish a related-party transaction. [3][4]
  • The year-over-year reduction in ECG balances is a useful indicator of lower recorded exposure, but not equivalent to a cash receipt record; IMF documentation of legacy-debt restructuring, foreign-exchange movements and state transfers makes causation uncertain. [7][8][9]

5.3 What we do not know [UNKNOWN]

No independently authenticated public evidence in the source corpus establishes Chai's natural-person UBOs; exact project share interests; seller(s); acquisition prices; fresh-capital versus secondary consideration ratio; target EBITDA, current net debt and debt-service coverage; actual dividend lockups; Bridge's precise FX PPA provisions; Chai preference dividend rate, currency, voting, priority or redemption terms; or regulatory approval and placement commitments. None is imputed. [18]

WSI independence accounting: KJ-01 has two distinct streams only for intended acquisition (issuer statement and original reporting), one original reporting stream for the US$300m amount, zero verified closing documents; KJ-02 has two sponsor disclosures corroborating the common Endeavor role but zero current complete cap tables; KJ-03 has two independent institutional streams (ECG auditor and IMF) supported by Ministry of Finance public disclosures; KJ-04 has one sponsor source on Twin City USD PPA, one media stream on Chai preference indexing and one independent ECG balance-sheet risk source, but zero confirmed legal matching terms. [1]–[12]

5.4 Binding constraints and key drivers

Drivers [FACT]: two operating IPPs; one disclosed common sponsor; state-linked ECG offtake; the CWM payment-routing system; a large recent reduction in target-specific recorded ECG payables. Constraints [FACT]: pending public SEC/GSE documentation; missing ownership and financing covenants; substantial sector funding gap. Drivers [INFERENCE]: preference-share cash obligations and shareholder-control incentives could influence distribution timing; regulatory changes and currency movements could accelerate or moderate financial stress. [1]–[12]

6.7 Transparent stress-test framework (not company forecasts)

Until audited target accounts and preference terms exist, the following are sensitivity identities, not predictions and not representations of Chai's actual financial position.

  • Project cash available for distribution (CFAD) = Cash PPA receipts − fuel and O&M cash − tax − required working capital − capex − scheduled debt service − mandatory reserve top-ups, subject to lender covenants.
  • Chai upstream cash = Σ (actual permitted dividends from each acquired stake) + any lawful other distributions, after withholding taxes.
  • Preference cash coverage = (Chai upstream cash − Chai overheads, taxes and reserve/hedge cash costs) / preference cash amount contractually due. Below 1.0x means the modeled cash flow alone cannot fund that payment. 1.0–1.2x is a proposed monitoring watch band, not a regulatory covenant.
  • Seller-exit ratio = Cash consideration paid to existing shareholders / gross proceeds. Primary project-capital ratio = Cash paid as new equity and directly funded project capex / gross proceeds. These metrics need a complete proceeds ledger and should not be assumed to sum to 100% because financing fees, reserves and refinancing also absorb cash.
  • Illustrative combined stress: If Chai's otherwise distributable upstream cash falls by 20%, a baseline 1.25x coverage ratio would fall to 1.00x (1.25 × 0.8). If preference distributions indexed to USD rise 15% in cedi terms with no matching hedge or incoming cash increase, that ratio would fall further to 0.87x (1.00 / 1.15). The 20%, 15% and 1.25x values are solely hypothetical demonstration assumptions, not current issuer metrics.

08 // IMPLICATIONS

Strategic Implications, Risk & Recommendations

8.1 Strategic implications

For Ghana's capital market: A listing could broaden infrastructure exposure and deepen participation, but instrument design, concentration and continuous secondary-market liquidity matter at least as much as gross issuance size. [1][13]

For Ghanaian institutional savers: The decisive rights are those of the actual preference instrument issued by Chai, not the project company's nominal MW capacity. Direct project ownership, holding-company preference shares and debt obligations occupy different legal positions.

For public finance and electricity security: ECG's payment capacity and supplementary sovereign financing can support project continuity but may transfer pressure to the fiscal account. The IMF identifies a still-large 2026 sector funding shortfall and a need for sustained regulatory adherence. [8][9][10]

For governance/Positive Peace and RCA/CPS: Accountable ownership and transparent allocation of infrastructure returns could improve domestic institutional participation; no conclusion of inclusive benefit follows merely from the word 'domestic'. A major preference offering must transparently distinguish who controls votes, who receives sale proceeds and who assumes operating risk.

8.3 Action thresholds and ownership

GREEN / MONITOR: Final published prospectus and authenticated cap tables; complete acquisition perimeter and beneficial ownership; independent valuations and disclosed use-of-proceeds split; project financials show sustainable post-debt distributions; currency matching adequately documented. Lead: transaction due-diligence analyst and corporate legal specialist.

AMBER / ENHANCED REVIEW: Missing or changed share class, seller opacity, material post-announcement amendments, preference cash coverage modeled below 1.20x, or marked deterioration in two consecutive target receivable reports. Lead: issuer diligence team, portfolio risk officer and QAP monitoring analyst.

RED / DEFER ANY POSITIVE INVESTABILITY JUDGMENT: No authenticated offer terms; unresolved issuer/seller related-party relationships; actual modeled coverage below 1.00x without funded reserves; breached lender distribution covenant; undisclosed material leverage or material unsupported claims of sovereign guarantees. Lead: risk committee and securities counsel. Thresholds are proposed internal decision rules, not quoted Ghanaian regulatory tests.

09 // CONFIDENCE

Confidence & Uncertainties

Composite confidence: MODERATE for sector and sponsor structure / LOW for issuance valuation and future shareholder returns. This division follows three constraints: (1) high-quality firsthand ECG and IMF records underpin sector exposure; (2) current sponsor statements establish selected legal connections and one specific ownership share; (3) the authenticated share purchase, valuation and preference-instrument documents needed to close the investment logic remain absent. Source quantity cannot overcome missing documents. [3][4][7][8][18]

Three potential judgment reversals: (i) prospectus shows most proceeds as new project capital rather than seller consideration, shifting KJ-01; (ii) issuer and target cap tables show no common seller and strong independent pricing, reducing concentration risk; (iii) target accounts and covenants reveal robust, matched upstream distributable cash and preference coverage, improving KJ-04. The reverse of each result would lower confidence in future dividends.

Assumption failure drill: If A3 fails (year-end payables declined mostly via FX adjustments or haircuts, or arrears return), the project payment narrative must be reestimated from cash receipts. If A4 fails (project debt restricts dividends), Chai's stake can be economically valuable yet unable to finance preference distributions; replace EBITDA proxies with legally available shareholder cash.

Release determination: This integrated report materially improves the analytical-completeness position versus the supplied Phase II research, but it cannot legitimately be designated a 95%+ evidentiary compliant, decision-grade valuation assessment until minimum primary records are available. Completeness of the QAP section structure is not the same thing as closure of transaction facts.
